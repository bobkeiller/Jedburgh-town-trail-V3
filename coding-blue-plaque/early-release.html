A prisoners-and-hats puzzle

Early Release

Ten captives, ten hidden hats and one strategy that can set everyone free.

The Bailiff’s challenge

The jail was full, with ten people locked inside. The Bailiff offered them a chance of early release.

  1. The captives stand in a row, one behind another, all facing forward.
  2. Each receives either a black or white hat, chosen with equal probability.
  3. No one can see their own hat or any hat behind them, but they can see everyone ahead.
  4. Starting with Player 10 at the back, each captive must say either “black” or “white”. Everyone hears the earlier answers.
  5. A correct guess earns release. If at least nine are correct, all ten go free.

Which strategy would you choose?

Player 10 counts the black hats in front. The prisoners agree beforehand that “white” means Player 10 sees an even number of black hats, while “black” means Player 10 sees an odd number. This answer carries information for the other nine prisoners; it is not simply a guess at Player 10’s own hat.

Example: Player 10 sees six black hats among Players 1–9, so says “white” to signal an even count. If Player 9 also sees six black hats ahead, Player 9 knows their own hat must be white. If Player 9 saw only five, they would know their own hat was black. Each following player uses Player 10’s signal together with the colours already deduced to identify their own hat.

Players 1–9 are therefore guaranteed to identify their hats correctly. Player 10 has a 50:50 chance of also naming their own hat correctly—but either way, at least nine are right, so the Bailiff’s rule releases all ten.

Without the special rule releasing everyone when nine answers are correct, parity would produce an average of 9.5 individually correct answers.
