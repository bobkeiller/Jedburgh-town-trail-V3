Weights and Measures

Five sacks, two kinds of coin and only one shilling to spend at the scales.

The Tolbooth scales

The Tolbooth once stood at the top of the Canongate, in the middle of the street. Among its services were official weights and measures: it could help weigh anything from a penny to a pony—for a cost, of course.

Your boss has given you five sacks of coins. Most are filled with convincing fakes, but one sack may contain genuine gold coins.

The coins look identical, the sacks feel much the same, and the scales cost one shilling per use. You have exactly one shilling. You may take out as many coins as you wish, provided you return them afterwards.

Your challenge: Choose how many coins to take from each sack. Your single weighing must let you prove which sack contains the genuine coins—without relying on guesswork or luck.
