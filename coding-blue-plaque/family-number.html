A High Street code puzzle

The Family Number

Can you recover the three-digit number that young Kevin Wood forgot?

Long before chip and PIN or contactless cards, a form of credit was used in the shops of Jedburgh High Street. In the sixteenth century, fewer than 1,000 families lived in and around Jedburgh. Each was given a unique three-digit number which they could use to obtain credit.

The traders included the Baxter (baker), Flesher (butcher), Webster (weaver) and Lorimer (maker of horse bits and bridles). It was a very cooperative arrangement. The number was known as the “Co-operative Number” or usually shortened to “Copey Number”.

Parents could send a child into town for a loaf and tell them to “put it on our copey number”. The child had to remember that number—and sometimes forgot.

On 14 November 1545, six-year-old Kevin Wood was sent to buy a loaf, but forgot his family number. Traders were not permitted to reveal numbers directly: the period’s form of cyber security. They could, however, allow five guesses and mark every digit in each incorrect attempt.

Kevin used all five guesses and went home empty-handed. Based on his failed guesses, can you work out the Wood family number?

How to play: The number contains three different digits. Green means the digit and position are correct; amber means the digit belongs elsewhere; red means the digit is not in the number. Select a position, enter three digits, then check your answer.
