Glebe Murder Part 4

The Courtroom

Solve one final piece of logic before the Sheriff closes the case.

The sergeant’s deduction

Police believed that the victim had been shot at close range with the gun from Des Dpuglas’s cabinet. When questioned on Sunday—a day when he always lied—Alan Renton denied taking the gun from Cupboard 2 and replacing it later.

Alan claimed that he had not used the gun, but had given it to a friend called Mick. When asked how many of his friends were called Mick, he blurted out:

“All of them!”

The logical police sergeant knew this statement was false. He wrote down five possibilities and realised that one of them had to be true.

Which option must be true?

If you want to know why, read the explanation

“All of my friends are called Mick” is a universal statement. Because Alan said it on a lying day, it must be false.

  • A must be true: Alan has at least one friend. If he had none, the claim would be a vacuous truth: there would be no friend who disproved it.
  • B need not be true: he might have several friends with different names.
  • C is false: having no friends would make his universal claim vacuously true.
  • D need not be true: none of his friends may be called Mick.
  • E need not be true: he could know both Mick and someone with another name.

So A is the only definitive conclusion. If Alan has just one friend, that friend cannot be called Mick.

This problem is based on a question from the 17th Brazilian Mathematical Olympiad of Public Schools.

The truth comes out

It turns out he only has one friend, called Spug, Spug Pringle — who had never even seen a shotgun and had a watertight alibi.

It’s not looking good for Renton — he has been caught — he had taken the gun.

The next day he was dragged in front of the Sheriff.

The Procurator Fiscal began.

Procurator Fiscal
Mr Renton, Mr Des Scapitachi Douglas left his house briefly to walk his dog at 19:00 and returned 15 minutes later. He would normally take the dog down to the Glebe but it was dreadful weather so he opted for a shorter route given it takes 10 minutes to reach the Glebe.
Procurator Fiscal
When he was out, did you take the gun?
Renton
Yes your Honour.
Procurator Fiscal
Where did you take it from?
Renton
Cupboard twae in Des’s front room, your Highness.
Procurator Fiscal
Why did you take it?
Renton
To scare some crows in a tree ahint ma house your Majesty.
Procurator Fiscal
When did you take it?
Renton
I saw Des taking his dog oot about 7pm, I nipped in efter that, err.. your Grace.
Procurator Fiscal
Where is your house?
Renton
Next door to Des’s. I stay there every time I am in town. We’re good mates — I know where he keeps the key for his gun cupboard, your Lordship.
Procurator Fiscal
Then what did you do?
Renton
I fired twae cartridges into the tree, the crows seemed to get the hint and all fu.., they all bug…, they all left abruptly, your Eminence. Then I put the gun back.
Renton
I would have asked Des to borrow the gun, but I ken that he worries that it could affect his licence, your Excellency.

After lots more questions and many more from Mr Renton’s lawyer, the Sheriff spoke privately with the Procurator Fiscal and the Defence Lawyer and summed up the evidence as follows.

The Sheriff’s conclusion

“The body of Mr Harry Bartholomew Page was found in the river at the Glebe at 09:15 on Saturday 20th of October 1923. Initial evidence suggested a shotgun assault at close range.

There were no reliable witnesses and no one reported hearing any shots being fired. The investigating officer suggested that the murderer could time his shots with thunder claps to disguise them.

This, frankly, is poppycock, no one can predict the timing of a nearby lightning strike and sharp thunder crack, and no one has the reactions to fire a shotgun at that exact moment.

The reason no one heard any shots is because there were no shots.

Harry Page was struck by lightning which threw him forward unconscious, where he drowned. The burns on his duffle coat are from lightning not a shotgun.

Mr Renton is a reliable witness on the days he tells the truth and an inveterate liar on other days.

He did not have the time to take the gun to the Glebe, shoot Page and return it — notwithstanding that Page was still alive at 23:00 when he left the Royal Hotel.

There was no murder — just a tragic accident.

Mr Renton, you are free to leave.”

Renton: “Thanks buddy.”
