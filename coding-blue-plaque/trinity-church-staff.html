Keeping the church going

This is not a murder mystery—it is a puzzle to discover how long each person had served at Jedburgh Trinity Church since it opened in 1818. The answer may be useful in another mystery later in the trail…

Although this site was once used by the Blackfriars, that is at odds with the convention that the “Trinitarians” were the Redfriars. The Whitefriars were known as the Carmelites—or “Caramel Lattes” to their detractors—and the Ayr Friars were handy cooks.

In 1850, records show that all five members of staff had been in place for different lengths of service.

Use the clues to arrange the staff from longest-serving to newest, then enter each person’s years of service.

How to play: Use the ↑ and ↓ buttons to put the staff in order. Enter a number of years beside every name, then select Check my answer.

The seven clues
  1. Boris had worked for seven-eighths of the time that Plumton had.
  2. Sandy had worked for half the time that Malcolm had.
  3. One year ago, Sandy had been working there for twice as many years as Eric has now.
  4. The longest-serving member of staff had now been there for 32 years.
  5. Plumton had been working there for ten years more than Malcolm.
  6. Eric was the newest member of staff.
  7. Five years after the date of the record, Sandy would have served for half the length of Plumton’s current tenure.

Your answer

Longest-serving → newest

Correct!

The service record is:

  1. Plumton — 32 years
  2. Boris — 28 years
  3. Malcolm — 22 years
  4. Sandy — 11 years
  5. Eric — 5 years

Keep those numbers in mind—you may meet them again later on the trail.
