Dancehall Pass-outs

Use Rob Keiller’s failed copies to uncover the pass-out pattern.

The young entrepreneur

In 1975, admission to the weekly Town Hall dance cost 50p—unless you bought a fake pass-out stamp from 11-year-old entrepreneur Rob Keiller. Using felt pens, Rob copied the stamp onto a customer’s hand for 20p, with a money-back guarantee if they did not get in.

20pRob’s pass-out

The organisers regularly changed the design and ink colour. Their latest trick used four coloured dots: one red, one blue, one black and one green.

On Saturday, 5 July 1975, Jedburgh marked the approaching 400th anniversary of the Raid of the Redeswire. Bruce Craigie was that year’s Callant, while Lord Home—formerly Prime Minister Sir Alec Douglas-Home—was the Redeswire Speaker. Coincidentally, he had been Prime Minister on the day Rob was born.

Rob tested patterns on four friends. Each returned with the number of dots that were in the correct position. Because every attempt used all four colours once, position was the only thing that mattered.

How to play: Select a colour, then select a position. Each colour can be used once. Fill all four positions and test your code.

What was the pass-out code?

Correct—green, red, blue, black

The first attempt proves that none of its colours is in the right position. Combining the two-position matches in the remaining attempts leaves only one possible order.

Rob redrew the correct pass-outs on his helpers’ hands. They mingled with the crowd, allowing him to sell another 25 copies and make £5.
