The Implausible Supper

Eight remarkable diners, one carefully arranged table—and a royal favourite hidden among them.

A most extraordinary guest list

In October 1605, a supper was held in Piper’s House by Alexander Seton. Four diners sat on one side of a simple rectangular table and four faced them on the other. Seton’s suppers were famous for the quality of both conversation and company—but this evening’s guest list was astonishing.

Meet the eight diners
Alexander Seton
Lord Chancellor of Scotland and host, diplomatically managing volatile rivals in the King’s unexpected presence.
William Shakespeare
The English playwright, quietly observing the table’s drama while gathering material for Macbeth.
Andrew Melville
A fiery Presbyterian preacher who opposed royal control over the Kirk—a theological time bomb.
John Napier
The eccentric Laird of Merchiston, absorbed in mathematics, astronomy and calculations concerning the Apocalypse.
Duncan Liddel
An Aberdeen mathematician and physician carrying the latest European astronomical charts.
Robert Kerr
Son of Sir Thomas Kerr, Laird of Ferniehirst Castle, just south of Jedburgh. At school he had the nickname "Wan".
Thomas Percy
Constable of Alnwick Castle and former secret intermediary in negotiations with King James VI. Also a conspirator in the Gunpowder Plot!
King James VI
The highly intellectual monarch who ruled the table, debated theology and guarded against hidden threats.

Some diners fervently supported the King; others questioned royal authority or plotted to topple the Protestant monarch. The King placed his most favoured guest immediately on his right—but who was it?

How to play: Select a diner from the waiting list, then select the seat where you think they belong. Select × to clear an individual seat. Seat everyone, then check your arrangement.

Reading the table: Seats are numbered clockwise. Seats 1–4 run left to right across the top; seats 5–8 run right to left across the bottom. A diner’s left and right are from their own seated point of view. Clues that say “on the diagram” use your point of view.

Clues

  1. Percy was sitting in an end seat.
  2. There were at least two people to the right of William Shakespeare on the diagram.
  3. Seton was sitting opposite Shakespeare and beside the man described as a ticking theological time bomb.
  4. The person on the King’s left had previously negotiated with the King.
  5. Liddel sat beside Melville, occupying one of the two middle seats on his side of the table.
  6. Seton sat in the highest-numbered seat.
  7. There was at least one person to Seton’s right on the diagram.
  8. Napier sat directly opposite Percy.
