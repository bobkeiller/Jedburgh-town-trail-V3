Cockburn’s Conundrum

Three bags. Three wrong labels. One piece of fruit is all you need—if you choose wisely.

Rob’s free-fruit challenge

Rob Cockburn was a friendly local grocer with a popular shop near Jedburgh’s Market Place. Always open to fun—especially when it increased sales—he placed three brown paper bags on his high counter.

One was labelled Apples, another Plums, and the third Apples and Plums. There was one bag of apples, one of plums and one mixed bag—but every label was wrong.

Children could pull one fruit from one bag, then try to identify all three. Most guessed incorrectly. Rob reclaimed the fruit with a manic “Bwuh-ha-ha!”—until David “Towser” Dick of Forthill Avenue discovered a method that never failed. Towser's mum Vera, known to her friends as "VD" would bring him into the shop and lift him onto a stool to make his guess.

Can you emulate Towser? Choose one bag, draw one fruit, and assign the correct contents to all three bags.

Towser’s Method

  1. Always choose the bag labelled Apples and Plums.
  2. Because every label is wrong, that bag cannot be mixed. The fruit you draw tells you its entire contents.
  3. Give the mixed label to the remaining bag whose displayed label does not match the fruit you drew.
  4. The final unused label belongs on the last bag.

Towser was not guessing. One carefully chosen fruit made the answer certain every time.
