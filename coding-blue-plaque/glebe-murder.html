An unsolved Jedburgh mystery

Glebe Murder Part 1

Three conflicting statements about a shotgun cupboard—only one of them is true.

The accusation

On 19 April 1923, a man was shot in the Glebe in Jedburgh. A witness said he had seen someone resembling Des Douglas carrying a shotgun near the Glebe that night.

Des was arrested and charged. He denied being anywhere near the Glebe and, although he owned a shotgun, and many other weapons, claimed he had not removed it from its locked cupboard for weeks.

There were three cupboards, numbered one, two and three. Des insisted that his gun had been locked away at the time of the murder, but could not remember exactly where. He gave three different statements:

  1. “It was in cupboard one.”
  2. “I know that it wasn’t in cupboard two.”
  3. “No, wait a minute, it wasn’t in cupboard one.”

Investigators established that exactly one of these three statements was true.

Where was the shotgun?

Test each possible cupboard against all three statements. Only one location makes exactly one statement true.

Correct—cupboard two

If the gun was in cupboard two, statement one is false, statement two is false, and statement three is true. That is the only option for which exactly one statement is true.

The outcome

Police found the gun in cupboard two. Although Des was an unreliable witness, and was known as "Mad Des", his shotgun was locked away and showed no sign of having been used. This helped prove his innocence.

Des Douglas was found not guilty. The Glebe murder was unsolved.
