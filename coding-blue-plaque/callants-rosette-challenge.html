A Jethart deduction puzzle

Callant’s Rosette Challenge

Listen carefully to two uncertain answers, then identify the rosette pinned to your own jacket.

The challenge

The colours of the Jethart Callants Festival are red and blue. You and two friends, Richie Nagle and Bryan Lyall, are visiting the Callants’ Club. They are both former Jethart Callants.

You are posed a challenge often given to visitors to the club.

A box contains three red rosettes and two blue rosettes.

All three of you close your eyes. A member of the bar staff selects a rosette for each of you and pins it to the back of your jackets. You can see the other two rosettes, but not your own—and nobody can see which two remain in the box.

The challenge is to be the first person who can state the colour of their own rosette with certainty. You can see that Bryan and Richie are both wearing red.

Bryan looks at you and Richie.He is asked to name his colour or wait until the others have spoken. “Pass,” he says.
Richie looks at you and Bryan.He is asked the same question. He is still unsure. “Pass,” he says.

What can you declare?

Choose only what the two passes allow you to know definitively.

Correct—you are wearing red

  1. If Bryan had seen two blue rosettes, he would immediately have known that his own must be red. He passed, so he did not see two blues.
  2. Richie hears Bryan pass. If your rosette were blue, Richie—who can see your rosette—would know that his own could not also be blue. Otherwise Bryan would have seen two blues and declared red.
  3. If yours were blue, Richie could therefore declare that he was red. But Richie also passes.
  4. Richie’s uncertainty rules out blue for your rosette. Your rosette must be red.
