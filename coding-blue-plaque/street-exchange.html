McDiarmid’s parlour trick

The Three Secret Letters

About a month after the Festival, George McDiarmid claimed he could reveal the mystery donor’s rank of peerage without breaking his promise to keep the man’s name secret.

There are five ranks of the peerage: Duke, Marquess, Earl, Viscount and Baron. In McDiarmid’s heraldic puzzle, each rank was represented by three letters:

DKEDuke
MARMarquess
ERLEarl
DELViscount
BARBaron

DEL was taken from the French equivalent, De Laviscont.

McDiarmid wrote the three letters of the donor’s abbreviation on three separate pieces of parchment. He folded them and handed one to each of three ladies. Each lady privately memorised her own letter.

McDiarmid to the first lady: “From your single letter alone, do you know the donor’s rank?”
First lady: “Yes.”
McDiarmid to the second lady: “You do not know the first lady’s letter. Having heard her answer, can you now identify the rank from your own letter?”
Second lady: “Yes.”

All three letters are different parchments from the same abbreviation. Each lady sees only her own letter, but hears the answers given before her.

What was the mystery donor’s rank?

Correct: Duke

The mystery donor was the Duke of Roxburgh.

McDiarmid’s deduction

  1. The first lady can know the rank from one letter only if her letter occurs in just one abbreviation.
  2. The unique letters are K in DKE, M in MAR and B in BAR. Her “Yes” therefore eliminates Earl and Viscount.
  3. If the rank were Marquess or Baron, the second lady would see A or R. Both MAR and BAR contain those letters, so she could not distinguish them.
  4. For Duke, the first lady must have K. The second lady then has D or E; after hearing the first answer, either identifies DKE.

Therefore Duke is the only rank compatible with both answers.
