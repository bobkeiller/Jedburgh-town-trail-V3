Welcome to Jedburgh

Audio guideListen to the introduction

Jedburgh is a town built on exceptional stories, exceptional people and a unique history.

It is the birthplace of people whose ideas helped us understand Neptune, stereoscopic vision and polarisation, and who invented rugby sevens. It was also home to one of the heroes who recovered secret codebooks from a sinking U-boat.

Wordsworth, Burns and Sir Walter Scott all visited. Kings and queens came here, and James Hutton began to realise that the Earth was much older than anyone knew.

Enjoy your time in our wonderful town. Talk to local people—we are very friendly—and let us know what you think.

How to use this trailRead the guide

Follow the locations in order or explore them at your own pace. Use the Location Map, written directions or the Walking directions button to travel between stops.

Audio starts switched on. You can turn it off at any time in Trail options.

Collecting the plaques

As you follow the trail, collect the historic blue plaques by pressing the “Found it” button beside each one. The plaques form seven collections. When asked to select a missing word or allocate a plaque to a theme, keep trying if your first answer is not correct.

Punishing Puzzle guidance

The puzzles include bespoke logic, list ordering, logic matrices, Murdoku, train tracks, codebreakers and seating plans. Work from facts you know, mark possibilities that can be ruled out, and combine clues rather than trying to solve each clue on its own.

Logic Matrix Puzzles

Mark direct matches and exclusions first. Every positive match eliminates the other choices in its row and column. Combine those eliminations until only one possibility remains.

Don’t solve each clue in isolation—the answer emerges by combining several small deductions.

Train Tracks Puzzles

Build one continuous railway from entrance to exit. Row and column numbers give the exact number of track squares. Track cannot branch, cross itself or end anywhere except the two endpoints.

Use the totals to identify where track must and cannot go, then let the route reveal itself gradually.

Codebreaker Puzzles

Compare similar guesses and note what changed. First identify which symbols belong in the code, then determine their positions. Check that every clue remains true.

Seating Puzzles

Start with definite positions, then combine clues such as “beside” and “opposite”. Each person has one seat and every seat has one person.
