# Jedburgh Town Trail — “This Week in Jedburgh’s Story” Audit

Audit date: 15 November 2026

## Purpose

This audit extracts people and calendar events from the 24 trail locations, story panels, the history timeline, blue-plaque material and linked puzzle narratives. It is intended to support a recurring “This Week in Jedburgh’s Story” marketing feature.

The safest editorial hierarchy is:

1. An exact-date Jedburgh event.
2. A verified birthday with a strong Jedburgh connection.
3. A month-only anniversary, clearly described as occurring “this month”.
4. A date-flexible story when no reliable anniversary falls that week.

An approximate date must never be presented as an exact anniversary. Fictional puzzle events should be marketed as trail challenges, not as history.

## Audit summary

- The history timeline contains approximately 58 named-person entries. Some entries contain couples, duplicate titles or multiple names.
- The wider trail adds further writers, architects, soldiers, scientists, local officials and puzzle characters.
- 21 birthday candidates currently have an exact or conventionally accepted date; several carry editorial caveats.
- 23 exact-date or tightly dated event candidates were extracted, including contextual and fictional-puzzle dates that must be labelled appropriately.
- Numerous medieval and local people have only a year, an approximate date or no established birth date.
- Several dated puzzle stories are fictional and must be labelled accordingly.

## F. Subjects to exclude or label carefully

### Fictional puzzle characters

The following should not enter the historical birthday database unless explicitly described as fictional:

- Kevin Wood and the “Family Number” story
- Des Douglas and the Glebe Murder puzzle cast
- Characters in “Murder in the Spread”
- The Bishop of Bonchester puzzle cast
- Other named suspects, witnesses or families created solely for trail challenges

### Sensitive historical subjects

- People accused of witchcraft: never turn accusations into entertainment or imply guilt.
- Murder victims, executed people and wartime deaths: use commemorative, not celebratory, language.
- Living or recently living local residents: confirm that biographical details are public and obtain consent where appropriate.
- Traditional stories: label them “tradition”, “legend” or “reputed”, matching the timeline’s evidence status.

## G. Duplicate and identity controls

The marketing database should merge these repeated identities:

- Charles III = Charles, Prince of Wales = Prince Charles, Duke of Rothesay.
- Charles Edward Stuart = Bonnie Prince Charlie = the Young Pretender.
- Robert I = Robert the Bruce.
- Mary, Queen of Scots = Queen Mary in the Jedburgh narrative; do not confuse her with Mary of Modena.
- Elizabeth II = Queen Elizabeth II; do not confuse her with Queen Elizabeth, later the Queen Mother.
- James VI of Scotland = James I of England.
